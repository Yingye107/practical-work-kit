# Relevant checks for everyday deliverables

Select checks that change the readiness decision; this is not a mandatory form.

**Messages and documents:** Is the audience clear? Is the intended action stated? Do dates, names, promises, and instructions agree? Is anything essential absent? Does a cited source support the actual claim? Respect the requested tone; a different writing style is not automatically an error. For an invitation, practical details matter more than elegant prose.

**Presentations and proposals:** Check the argument, supporting evidence, numbers, and what the audience is being asked to do. Distinguish an unsupported business assumption from an internal inconsistency. A text-only inspection cannot establish whether rendered slides are readable; inspect the rendering when available and material.

**Budgets, tables, and schedules:** Recompute relevant totals, quantities, unit prices, and date ranges. Check currencies, tax treatment, time zones, units, and whether categories are missing or double-counted when they affect the purpose. If source data or formula access is unavailable, state precisely what was and was not checked. Avoid false precision and invented costs.

**Designs and instructions:** Check whether the intended audience can perform the main action and whether the artifact contains the necessary information. Claim measured contrast, mobile behavior, or actual usability only with the corresponding evidence. A screenshot can reveal a visible issue, but does not prove live interaction works.
